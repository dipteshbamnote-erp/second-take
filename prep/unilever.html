How it works

Our promise

Six things Second Take will never do

We wrote these before we wrote anything else. When a feature request conflicts with one of them, the request loses.

1

Never claim to know what a specific company will ask

"Leaked" question lists are guesses, go stale fast, and train memorisation. We give you practice prompts of the same type instead. Company mode follows the interview format each company publishes and links to it, but every prompt is our own.

2

Never write or rewrite your answer

A memorised answer collapses when the interviewer changes one word. We show you what's missing; you do the rewrite.

3

Never predict whether you'll be selected

Hiring depends on far more than one answer. We tell you how complete your structure is, nothing more.

4

Never pass off practice prompts as real company material

Every prompt is written for practice and labelled that way. We don't represent any company, and we never promise a question will come up in a company's interview.

5

Never send your personal details anywhere

We ask only for your name, email, college and batch, to personalise your practice. They're kept with your progress in your browser and in a private backup that only your recovery code (or, on claude.ai, your own Claude account) can open. We never sell or share them, never ask for a CV, phone number or roll number, and you can delete your backup any time.

6

Never give feedback it can't point to

Every tick and gap names the rubric element and the words that counted. No vague "great answer!"

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is it really free?

Yes. Everything on the site today is free during the beta. Before practising you add a short profile (name, email, college and batch). It stays in your browser, and there's no password or payment.

Which fields does it cover?

Product management, consulting and strategy, marketing, finance and banking, operations and supply chain, HR, business analytics and general management. Pick your field and the rounds, prompts and feedback change to match. You can switch any time.

Can it tell me what McKinsey or Google will ask?

No, and that's deliberate. Nobody reliably knows, and memorising rumoured questions makes you brittle when the real prompt differs. Practise the type instead.

Where do the question bank questions come from?

We write them ourselves. They are standard, widely asked interview questions, phrased in Second Take's own words, so we can mark them as ours. We don't copy question banks from other websites or books, because most are copyrighted. For each company, we link to the free prep material it publishes on its own careers site instead of copying it.

How many practice prompts are there?

Over 2.3 million. Each round type has templates written for that domain, combined with lists of industries, users, cities, apps and realistic numbers. So many prompts share a pattern but differ in context, and every case prompt comes with interviewer facts whose numbers add up. Every prompt has an ID, like #CS-1A2B. When you want something completely new, press "Fresh prompt from Claude" for a brand-new prompt written for your round.

Are you connected to these companies?

No. Second Take is an independent practice tool. It does not represent, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by, any company named here. Company names are used only to describe how each company publicly says it interviews. We do not promise that these questions, or questions like them, will be asked in that company's interview.

How does company mode work?

For 40 recruiters, we read how each company describes its own interviews on its official careers pages, and built practice rounds in that format. For example, Amazon's rounds check for its Leadership Principles and STAR with measurable results, and Deloitte's case follows its published five steps. Each company page shows the source link. The prompts are written by Second Take; they are not the company's actual questions.

Why won't it just show me a good answer?

Because reading a polished answer feels like learning but isn't. You get better by finding your own gap and fixing it, which is exactly what each take makes you do.

Where are my profile and progress stored?

In your browser, and in a private backup so you don't lose them. On 2secondtake.com, your profile and practice results (scores and which elements you missed) are backed up to private storage under a random recovery code that only you hold. Use the code to restore everything on a new phone or laptop. On claude.ai, your progress is saved privately to your own Claude account where your account allows it. The answers you type or speak are never stored; they're only sent to Claude when you ask for an expert review. You can delete your backup any time from My progress.

I lost my phone or cleared my browser. Is my progress gone?

No. On 2secondtake.com, open Sign in and choose “Restore your progress”, then enter your recovery code. You'll find the code on My progress, and you can email it to yourself from there. Keep it private, because anyone with the code can see your practice history. We don't use passwords, so we can't recover a lost code.

How does "Speak your answer" work?

Where your browser allows it, for example Chrome or Edge on a site you open directly, Second Take listens and types your words as you speak. Where the microphone is blocked, including inside some embedded viewers, the Speak button switches to your device's own voice typing: Windows + H on Windows, the Fn key twice on a Mac, or the microphone key on a phone keyboard. Either way, your answer counts as spoken and gets a filler-word check.

How do I get the expert review on 2secondtake.com?

Connect your own Claude, in one of two ways. Choose “Open in Claude” to copy your answer and open Second Take inside Claude, where the review runs free on your Claude account. Or paste an Anthropic API key from console.anthropic.com to run the review right on the website; the key stays in your browser, is sent only to Anthropic, and usage is billed to your Anthropic account. You can disconnect it any time from your profile.

Is the feedback from AI?

Yes, in two layers. An instant checklist marks which parts of a strong answer you covered and shows the words that counted. Then an expert review by Claude reads your answer against what top interviewers expect in that domain, such as the 5 Cs for a credit case or a MECE profit tree for a business case. It quotes your exact words for every point, checks your numbers against the case facts, and gives a follow-up question and a drill. Quotes are checked against your answer, and any that don't match are dropped. The review never rewrites your answer. It runs on your own Claude account, so Claude asks your permission the first time.

Can I ask the interviewer questions?

In case rounds (consulting, marketing, operations, HR and credit cases), yes: up to two, just like clarifying with a real interviewer. Ask about the goal, costs, prices, competitors and so on. If the case doesn't have that information, the coach tells you to make an assumption. Asking a good question counts toward "Clarify the goal and scope".

Can it replace mocks with seniors?

No. Seniors judge depth, insight and presence, which Second Take can't. Use it to fix structural gaps so your senior mock goes on the harder stuff.

A note from our founder

Why I built Second Take

Nobody should walk into the most important conversation of their year without hearing the truth about their answer first.

Dear future interviewee,

Placement season does something strange to people. Classmates who argue brilliantly in class go quiet in the corridor outside an interview room. Everyone has read the same frameworks. Everyone has a folder of questions. And almost nobody knows whether their answers are actually good.

That was the part that stayed with me. Not a lack of preparation, but a lack of honest feedback. Seniors are generous, but they have their own deadlines, and a good mock slot is gold. Friends are kind, and kindness is the one thing you don't need the night before an interview. So most of us practise into a mirror, or into silence, and only find out what we missed when an interviewer leans back and says, “Okay, let's move on.”

I built Second Take for the moment before the real one. A place where you can say your answer out loud at midnight and hear, plainly, what was missing. Where the feedback points to your own words, not to a template. Where you get to try again, and watch yourself get better.

I built it around a few promises I care about more than any feature. It will never write your answer for you, because the person who walks into that room has to own every word. It will never pretend to know what a company will ask. And it will never tell you whether you'll get the job, because no tool should have that much say in how you feel about yourself.

Every great answer you've ever heard was somebody's second take. The first one was rough, unsure and full of gaps. What changed it was someone showing them where to look.

I hope Second Take does that for you. And I hope that when you walk into that room, you walk in calm, because you've already heard the truth about your answer and made it better.

With you in this,

DipteshFounder, Second Take · PGPM, Great Lakes Institute of Management

Problem-framing memo

ToProduct review, PPRO002FromDipteshRePractice feedback for MBA placement interviews

The real problem

MBA students preparing for placement interviews, whether in product, consulting, marketing, finance, operations, HR or analytics, get too few rounds of specific feedback on the structure of their reasoning before placements: clarifying before solving, segmenting users, prioritising, defining success, landing a recommendation. Seniors' mock slots are scarce and peers' feedback is inconsistent and often polite. Students repeat the same structural gaps and discover them only in the real interview.

False problems

  • "Students lack question banks." Questions are everywhere. More questions without feedback means more unexamined practice.
  • "Students don't know frameworks." Most can name CIRCLES. The gap is applying one under pressure to an unfamiliar prompt.
  • "Students need to know what Company X will ask." The most-voiced demand, and the one to refuse: it rewards memorisation and can't be verified.
  • "Students lack confidence." A symptom of not knowing whether an answer is good. Credible feedback treats the cause.

Who, and when

First- and second-year MBA students in the 4–8 weeks before placement interviews, mostly practising alone in the evening.

Unknowns

  • Does better structure in practice transfer to a live interview under pressure?
  • Will students trust machine feedback over a senior's, especially critical feedback?
  • Does the rubric match what recruiters reward, or do they weigh insight more?
  • How many retries does it take before a gap stops recurring?
  • Will students stay with a coach that refuses to write answers when a general chatbot will?

What would change my mind

If recruiters say structure is rarely why candidates fail, the problem is mis-framed and the product should shift to content or delivery.

AI-worthiness

Answers are free-form speech: the same idea appears in many phrasings and orders, so judging them is a language task, and a self-ticked checklist only tests the self-assessment students lack. Errors are cheap because it's practice: a wrong critique costs one retry, and no hiring decision depends on it. AI is not needed for serving prompts, timing or tracking progress. It earns its place in the critique step, which now has two layers: a transparent keyword checklist that runs instantly and can be audited, and an expert review by Claude constrained by the never list. Every point must quote the answer, and the page drops any quote it can't find, so the model can't invent evidence.

Behaviour spec · the never list is on Our promise, written first

Intended

  • Serves a labelled practice prompt for the chosen type.
  • Marks each rubric element covered or missing, with the evidence phrase.
  • Flags order problems.
  • Ends with one coaching question; shows the change on retry.
  • Accepts typed or spoken answers; counts filler words in spoken ones.
  • Sign-in profile (name, email, college, batch) personalises the progress tracker; progress is kept per profile in the browser and backed up privately (a recovery-code-keyed private store on the website, the person's own Claude account on claude.ai). Answers themselves are never stored.
  • Expert review: domain-specific feedback with scores on 4 dimensions, gaps ordered by impact, an arithmetic and facts check, a follow-up question and a drill.
  • The student picks one of 8 fields; rounds, prompts and rubrics are tailored to it.
  • Over 2.3 million generated prompts from domain templates, each with an ID; case prompts get consistent interviewer facts; Claude can write fresh prompts on demand.
  • Question bank: about 200 standard questions written in Second Take's own words and watermarked, with new Technical concepts and HR & fit rounds; company material is linked, never copied.
  • Case rounds: the candidate may ask the interviewer up to two questions. Replies come only from fixed case facts; anything else is "not available, make an assumption".
  • Tracks recurring gaps on the device.

Refusal

  • Names the request, gives a one-sentence reason, offers the nearest useful alternative.
  • Never ends the session.
  • Triggers: company-question prediction, rewrites or model answers, selection prediction.
  • The expert reviewer gets the never list as hard rules; hints describe what to add, never phrasing. The page drops any quote not found in the answer.
  • Company mode: uses only formats a company publishes itself, links the source, and labels every prompt as Second Take's own.

Failure

  • No rubric elements: flags possible off-topic.
  • Personal data: asks for removal, processes nothing.
  • All elements present: the expert review judges depth; a senior is still the final judge.
  • Expert review unavailable or declined: says so plainly; the instant checklist still works.
  • Mic blocked: switches to the device's own voice typing with OS-specific steps; typing always works.
